Bivy vs. cloud coding-agent queues

Cloud agents optimize for zero setup: upload or connect a repository, use the vendor's agent on the vendor's compute, and pay through that vendor. Bivy makes the opposite trade: coordination is hosted if you want it, but agents execute on infrastructure you control, with the agent, model credentials, and cloud provider you choose.

The short version. Choose a cloud queue when bundled compute and minimal setup matter most. Choose Bivy when agent choice, your existing toolchain, infrastructure control, and avoiding compute/token markup matter more.

What Bivy is not

Not bundled inference

You bring a model API key or an existing provider login. Bivy does not resell model tokens.

Not zero infrastructure

You need a machine or cloud account. Bivy can launch ephemeral capacity, but it remains your account and bill.

Not only a phone remote

The app is a control surface. The product is the queue, routing, fleet session layer, ephemeral runners, and webhooks around it.

Compared with first-party remote control

Anthropic and OpenAI provide excellent remote experiences for their own agents. If your entire workflow is one vendor and you do not need queue routing, generic webhooks, BYO-cloud runners, or cross-agent session mobility, a first-party remote may be the simpler choice. Bivy is for mixed-agent workflows and infrastructure you want to control.

Compared with SSH, tmux, and a VPN

That stack remains a strong free option for terminal access. Bivy adds a structured mobile chat client, diffs and approvals, push notifications, an issue-to-PR queue, node-and-agent routing, ephemeral runner orchestration, and session fork/move. It coordinates work rather than replacing your shell.
